# Stick Built vs Unitized Curtain Walls: Key Differences, Pros, Cons and Performance

Stick-built curtain walls are assembled on-site using individual framing members and glazing components installed piece by piece. In contrast, unitized curtain walls are factory-assembled into large pre glazed panels and installed as complete units. The primary differences involve installation speed, quality control, cost structure, and high-rise performance capability.

## What Is a Stick Built Curtain Wall?

A stick-built curtain wall system is a façade system constructed on-site using separate vertical and horizontal framing members. Individual components such as mullions, transoms, and glass panels are delivered to the construction site and assembled sequentially. This system offers flexibility but requires more field labor and installation time.

### Key Characteristics of a Stick-Built Curtain Wall System
- On-site assembly of framing members
- Field-installed glazing
- Sequential floor-by-floor construction
- Lower initial fabrication cost
- Higher dependence on site labor
- Greater flexibility for design adjustments

### Typical Applications of Stick Built Curtain Wall Systems

Stick-built systems are commonly used in:
- Low-rise commercial buildings
- Mid-rise office developments
- Renovation projects
- Buildings with complex or irregular geometries
- Projects with limited prefabrication access

Because installation occurs on-site, this system adapts more easily to design changes during construction.

## What Is a Unitized Curtain Wall?

A unitized curtain wall system consists of large, factory-assembled façade panels that integrate framing, glazing, insulation, and sealing components. These panels are transported to the construction site and installed as modular units, typically spanning one floor in height and one structural bay in width.

This prefabrication approach improves installation speed and ensures consistent quality control.

### Key Characteristics of a Unitized Curtain Wall System
- Off-site factory fabrication
- Pre-glazed panels
- Integrated insulation and gaskets
- Reduced on-site labor
- Faster installation schedule
- Engineered interlocking stack joints

### Typical Applications of Unitized Curtain Wall Systems

Unitized systems are most suitable for:
- High-rise office towers
- Mixed-use developments
- Hospitals and institutional buildings
- Large-scale commercial construction
- Projects with compressed timelines

Because panels arrive preassembled, this system minimizes weather-related installation delays and enhances performance reliability.

## Core Structural Components in Stick Built vs Unitized Curtain Walls Systems

Although installation methods differ, both systems rely on similar structural elements within the building envelope system.

### Primary Framing Components
- Vertical mullions – Transfer wind loads and support glazing
- Horizontal transoms – Connect mullions and support glass units
- Spandrel panels – Conceal floor slabs and mechanical systems
- Vision glass units – Provide daylight and exterior visibility

### Anchoring and Structural Integration
- Anchorage systems – Connect curtain wall to structural slab
- Dead load anchors – Support vertical weight
- Wind load anchors – Resist lateral forces
- Thermal breaks – Reduce thermal bridging
- Stack joints – Allow vertical movement between panels

In stick-built systems, these components are assembled sequentially on-site. In unitized systems, they are integrated into pre-engineered modules before delivery.

## Performance Differences Between Stick Built Curtain Walls and Unitized Curtain Walls

Performance is one of the most critical decision factors when selecting a curtain wall system. While both systems can meet modern building codes, their method of fabrication significantly affects consistency, durability, and structural behavior.

### Air Infiltration Performance

Air infiltration performance measures how much uncontrolled air passes through the building envelope system.

A unitized curtain wall system generally provides superior air tightness because factory-installed gaskets, sealants, and interlocking joints are assembled under controlled conditions. In contrast, a stick-built curtain wall system relies heavily on field sealing, which increases variability depending on weather conditions and installer precision.

Key influencing elements:
- Factory-installed gasket systems
- Pressure-equalized joints
- Sealant continuity
- ASTM E283 air leakage testing

### Water Penetration Resistance

Water penetration resistance is essential for façade durability and interior protection.

In unitized systems, integrated drainage paths and pressure-equalized rainscreen design improve water management. Because panels are pre-assembled, joint tolerances are more precise. Stick-built systems can also perform well, but field-applied sealants increase the risk of long-term leakage if not installed correctly.

Testing standards include:
- ASTM E331 water penetration test
- AAMA water resistance standards
- Dynamic water infiltration testing

### Thermal Performance and Energy Efficiency

Thermal performance depends on glazing selection, insulation continuity, and thermal break integration.

Both systems can achieve similar U-values and Solar Heat Gain Coefficient (SHGC) ratings when designed properly. However, unitized curtain walls often demonstrate improved thermal bridging control because insulation and thermal barriers are installed in a factory environment with tighter tolerances.

Important thermal considerations:
- Continuous thermal breaks
- Low-E glass coatings
- Double glazing or triple glazing
- Condensation resistance factor
- NFRC certification

### Structural Performance and Wind Load Resistance

Structural performance becomes critical in high-rise applications where wind pressures increase significantly.

A unitized curtain wall system is typically better suited for tall buildings due to engineered stack joints, integrated interlocking mullions, and tested interstory drift accommodation. These systems are designed to handle vertical building movement and lateral loads more efficiently.

In comparison, a stick-built curtain wall system can perform adequately in low- to mid-rise buildings but may require additional reinforcement in high-rise structures.

Structural engineering considerations include:
- Wind load resistance
- Seismic drift compatibility
- Deflection limits (L/175, L/240)
- Load path analysis
- Finite element analysis

## Stick Built and Unitized Curtain Walls Installation Process Differences

Installation methodology is one of the most visible distinctions between the two systems.

### Installation Process for a Stick-Built Curtain Wall System

The stick-built installation process involves assembling vertical mullions first, followed by horizontal transoms, and finally installing glazing panels.

Steps typically include:
- Anchor installation at slab edge
- Vertical mullion placement
- Horizontal transom connection
- Field glazing installation
- Sealant application and weatherproofing

Because work is completed floor-by-floor in open conditions, the process is more exposed to weather and site constraints.

### Installation Process for a Unitized Curtain Wall System

The unitized installation process relies on crane-assisted placement of pre-glazed panels.

Typical sequence:
- Anchor bracket installation
- Delivery of factory-assembled panels
- Crane lifting and placement
- Interlocking stack joint connection
- Minimal field sealing

Since panels arrive preassembled, installation progresses faster and reduces field error risk.

## Engineering Standards and Testing Requirements

Both systems must comply with rigorous performance standards to ensure long-term durability and safety.

### Primary Testing Standards
- ASTM E283 – Air leakage testing
- ASTM E331 – Static water penetration testing
- ASTM E1105 – Dynamic water testing
- AAMA performance standards
- NFRC thermal certification

### Mock-Up Testing and Quality Control

Before installation, large projects often require performance mock-up testing.

This includes:
- Structural load testing
- Water infiltration testing
- Air leakage verification
- Seismic movement simulation

In a unitized curtain wall system, quality control occurs primarily during factory production. In a stick-built curtain wall system, quality control is divided between fabrication and field installation.

## Advantages and Disadvantages of Stick-Built Curtain Wall Systems

Understanding the strengths and limitations of a stick-built curtain wall system helps determine whether it aligns with project size, budget, and design complexity.

### Advantages of a Stick-Built Curtain Wall
- Lower initial fabrication cost
- Greater on-site design flexibility
- Easier material transportation logistics
- Suitable for phased construction
- Adaptable to irregular geometries

Because assembly occurs on-site, modifications can be accommodated during installation without major manufacturing revisions.

### Disadvantages of a Stick-Built Curtain Wall
- Longer installation duration
- Higher dependence on field labor
- Greater exposure to weather delays
- Increased variability in sealant quality
- Higher risk of inconsistent air infiltration performance

While cost-effective in smaller buildings, performance predictability may vary based on installer expertise and environmental conditions.

## Advantages and Disadvantages of Unitized Curtain Wall Systems

A unitized curtain wall system emphasizes factory precision and installation efficiency, particularly in large-scale projects.

### Advantages of a Unitized Curtain Wall
- Faster installation speed
- Improved factory-controlled quality
- Enhanced air and water performance
- Better suitability for high-rise structures
- Reduced site labor intensity
- Improved schedule predictability

Factory fabrication reduces variability and allows detailed inspection before panels reach the job site.

### Disadvantages of a Unitized Curtain Wall
- Higher upfront manufacturing investment
- Early design finalization required
- Dependence on crane access
- Transportation constraints for large panels
- Less flexibility for late-stage design changes

Although more expensive initially, risk mitigation and time savings can offset cost in high-rise developments.

## When to Choose Stick Built Curtain Walls vs Unitized Curtain Walls

Selecting the appropriate curtain wall system depends on building height, construction schedule, labor market conditions, and architectural requirements.

### Choose a Stick-Built Curtain Wall System When:
- The building is low-rise or mid-rise
- Budget constraints prioritize lower upfront costs
- The façade includes complex geometry
- Construction sequencing requires flexibility
- Crane access is limited

### Choose a Unitized Curtain Wall System When:
- The project is a high-rise development
- Installation speed is critical
- Labor availability is limited
- Performance consistency is a priority
- Weather exposure risk must be minimized

In general, unitized systems are more efficient for tall commercial towers, while stick-built systems remain viable for smaller-scale projects.

## Sustainability and Energy Efficiency Considerations

Modern building codes emphasize energy efficiency and environmental responsibility. Both systems can meet sustainability goals, but fabrication methods influence performance consistency.

### Energy Performance Factors
- Thermal break continuity
- Low-E glazing systems
- Double or triple glazing
- Air barrier integration
- Pressure-equalized rainscreen design

A unitized curtain wall system often achieves more consistent energy performance because insulation and sealing components are installed under controlled factory conditions.

### Environmental Impact Considerations

Key sustainability metrics include:
- Embodied carbon of aluminum framing
- Recyclable material content
- Operational energy performance
- LEED certification contribution
- Building envelope commissioning

While both systems use similar materials, reduced on-site waste and improved manufacturing efficiency can enhance the environmental profile of a unitized façade system.
