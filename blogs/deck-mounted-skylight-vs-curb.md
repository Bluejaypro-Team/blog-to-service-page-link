# Curb Mounted vs Deck Mounted Skylight: Which is Better for Waterproofing?

A curb mounted skylight installs on a raised roof curb, making it ideal for flat roofs and low-slope roofs where water drainage is a concern. A deck mounted skylight attaches directly to the roof deck, creating a lower-profile design best suited for pitched roofs and residential applications.

## What Is a Curb Mounted Skylight?

A curb mounted skylight is designed to sit on top of a raised framed curb built above the roof surface. The curb is typically constructed from pressure-treated lumber, metal framing, or engineered composite materials.

The skylight unit attaches to this raised platform, and the surrounding area is sealed using a flashing system and roofing membrane. Because the skylight sits above the roofline, it provides additional protection against standing water and drainage issues.

This design is commonly used on flat commercial roofs, where water runoff is slower and elevation is critical for preventing leaks.

## What Is a Deck Mounted Skylight?

A deck mounted skylight installs directly onto the roof decking, eliminating the need for a separate curb. The skylight frame is secured to the structural opening and sealed with a factory-designed flashing kit.

Because it integrates tightly with the roofing material, this system creates a streamlined appearance and minimizes visible bulk. It is typically used on asphalt shingle roofs, metal roofs, and other steep-slope roofing systems.

The lower profile reduces wind exposure and enhances aesthetic appeal on residential properties.

## Which Skylight Is Better for Flat Roofs?

A curb mounted skylight is generally the better option for a flat roof or low-slope roof system. The raised curb structure helps prevent water intrusion by elevating the glazing above areas where water may collect.

Many local building codes require minimum curb heights for low-slope installations. This ensures proper drainage and reduces long-term moisture damage risks.

A deck mounted skylight is not typically recommended for flat roof applications unless specifically engineered for that purpose.

## Which Skylight Is Best for Pitched Roofs?

A deck mounted skylight is usually the preferred solution for a pitched roof or steep-slope roof system. The direct integration with roofing materials provides a clean exterior finish and reduces installation bulk.

Because water naturally sheds quickly on steep slopes, the need for elevated curb protection decreases. The integrated flashing system simplifies waterproofing and improves installation efficiency.

For residential construction, deck-mounted models are often the standard choice.

## Structural Performance Considerations

The structural difference between a raised curb assembly and a direct deck attachment affects more than appearance. It influences wind resistance, water management, and long-term durability.

A properly constructed curb assembly can improve water deflection in heavy rainfall conditions. Meanwhile, a tightly sealed deck-mounted frame may reduce minor air leakage points due to fewer structural transitions.

Both systems perform reliably when installed according to manufacturer specifications and local code requirements.

## Deck Mounted Skylight vs Curb Replacement and Maintenance Differences

One major advantage of a curb mounted skylight system is simplified replacement. Because the roof curb remains intact, installers can remove the old skylight and mount a new unit without major structural changes.

In contrast, replacing a deck mounted skylight may require removing surrounding roofing shingles or panels. This can increase labor costs and extend installation time.

Maintenance for both systems typically involves inspecting the flashing components, checking for seal failure, and monitoring interior condensation.

## Which Skylight Is More Energy Efficient?

Energy efficiency depends more on glazing technology and installation quality than on mounting style alone. However, differences between a curb mounted skylight and a deck mounted skylight can influence thermal performance.

A deck mounted skylight typically creates a tighter seal against the roof deck, which may slightly reduce air infiltration. A curb mounted system, if improperly insulated around the curb cavity, can introduce minor thermal bridging.

When comparing performance, focus on measurable ratings such as U-factor, Solar Heat Gain Coefficient (SHGC), and Visible Transmittance (VT) rather than mounting type alone.

## Deck vs Curb Mounted Skylight Thermal Performance Metrics

#### U-Factor

The U-factor measures how much heat transfers through the skylight assembly. Lower values indicate better insulation and reduced heat loss during cold weather.

Both curb mounted units and deck mounted units are available with low U-factor ratings when equipped with advanced insulated glass units (IGUs).

#### Solar Heat Gain Coefficient (SHGC)

The Solar Heat Gain Coefficient measures how much solar radiation passes through the glazing. Lower SHGC values reduce unwanted heat gain in hot climates.

Mounting style has minimal impact on SHGC. Instead, Low-E coatings, tinted glass, and laminated glazing layers control solar heat performance.

#### Thermal Bridging

Thermal bridging occurs when conductive materials allow heat transfer across the building envelope. In some curb mounted skylight installations, insufficient curb insulation may increase this risk.

Properly insulating the curb structure and sealing with high-quality air barriers significantly reduces thermal bridging concerns.

## Cost Comparison: Curb Mounted vs Deck Mounted Skylight

Installation cost varies based on roof type, labor rates, and project complexity. However, there are general cost trends between a curb mounted skylight installation and a deck mounted skylight installation.

### Initial Installation Cost

A curb mounted skylight may require additional labor to construct or modify the roof curb. This can increase upfront installation cost, especially if a new curb must be framed.

A deck mounted skylight eliminates the curb construction step but may require precise integration with surrounding roofing materials.

On flat commercial roofs, curb-mounted models are often more cost-effective due to standardized installation methods.

### Replacement Cost

Replacement is typically easier and less expensive with a curb mounted system. Since the curb frame remains in place, installers often replace only the skylight unit.

Replacing a deck mounted skylight may require partial removal of roof shingles or panels. This can increase labor time and material cost.

### Long Term Maintenance Cost

Maintenance costs for both systems depend largely on flashing durability, seal integrity, and climate exposure.

A poorly sealed curb assembly may require occasional re-sealing around the flashing joints. Meanwhile, deck-mounted systems may require periodic inspection of integrated flashing kits and sealants.

## Leak Risk: Which Skylight Is Less Likely to Leak?

Neither mounting style is inherently more prone to leaks. Most skylight leaks occur due to improper installation, poor flashing design, or degraded roofing materials.

A curb mounted skylight relies heavily on proper installation of the flashing system around the curb perimeter. If flashing is poorly integrated, water intrusion may occur.

A deck mounted skylight depends on correct placement of the manufacturer-provided flashing kit and proper shingle layering.

Professional installation and compliance with local building codes are the most critical leak-prevention factors.

## Waterproofing Systems

### Flashing System

A flashing system is a protective barrier that directs water away from the skylight opening. Both mounting types require high-quality flashing for long-term performance.

Curb-mounted systems may use custom-fabricated continuous flashing or manufacturer-specified kits. Deck-mounted systems typically rely on integrated flashing components engineered for specific roof pitches.

### Ice and Water Shield

An ice and water shield membrane is a self-adhering waterproof layer installed around roof penetrations. It provides secondary protection beneath the primary flashing system.

Both curb mounted installations and deck mounted installations should include this membrane to reduce the risk of leaks caused by ice dams or wind-driven rain.

### Roof Pitch and Drainage

Roof pitch significantly affects water drainage. A low-slope roof benefits from the elevation provided by a raised curb structure.

On steep slopes, rapid water runoff makes a deck mounted skylight sufficient without additional elevation.

Improper pitch compatibility is one of the leading causes of premature skylight failure.

- Limited compatibility with low-slope applications

## Which Skylight Type Is Best for Different Climates?

Climate plays a major role in choosing between a curb mounted skylight and a deck mounted skylight. Factors such as rainfall intensity, snowfall accumulation, temperature swings, and wind exposure affect long-term performance.

A cold climate region benefits from skylights with low U-factor ratings, insulated glazing, and proper curb insulation. In snowy areas, a raised curb structure may help reduce moisture intrusion caused by ice dam formation.

In hot climates, a skylight with a low Solar Heat Gain Coefficient and advanced Low-E glass coating is more important than mounting style. Both curb-mounted and deck-mounted models can perform efficiently when properly specified.

## Performance in Heavy Rainfall Areas

In regions with frequent storms, proper flashing integration is critical. A curb mounted system provides elevation above the roof plane, which can reduce exposure to pooling water on low-slope roofs.

A deck mounted skylight, when installed on a steep roof with a manufacturer-approved flashing kit, sheds water effectively due to natural gravity drainage.

Roof pitch compatibility is often more important than mounting category in high-rainfall environments.

## Performance in Snow and Ice Conditions

Snow accumulation increases structural load and moisture risk. A properly built roof curb assembly can elevate glazing above melting snow and improve water diversion.

However, insufficient insulation around a curb can increase the risk of condensation buildup. Proper air sealing and thermal insulation are essential in cold-weather installations.

A deck-mounted model on a steep roof typically benefits from faster snow shedding.

## Building Code and Compliance Considerations

Local and national building regulations often influence skylight selection. Many jurisdictions follow standards outlined in the International Residential Code (IRC) or International Building Code (IBC).

For low-slope installations, codes frequently require a minimum curb height requirement to ensure proper water drainage. This makes curb-mounted models the compliant choice for many commercial flat roofs.

In hurricane-prone regions, skylights may need impact-resistant glazing and specific wind uplift ratings regardless of mounting style.

Always verify compliance with local building authorities before installation.

## Lifespan Comparison

The lifespan of a curb mounted skylight system or deck mounted skylight system depends on glazing quality, installation accuracy, and climate exposure.

Most high-quality skylights last between 20 and 30 years when properly maintained. Mounting style alone does not significantly change expected durability.

However, a well-constructed curb frame can extend functional life by allowing easier unit replacement without disturbing surrounding roofing materials.

Regular inspection of flashing components, sealants, and insulated glass units helps maximize lifespan.

## Final Recommendation

Choose a curb mounted skylight system for flat or low-slope roofs, commercial applications, and situations where easier future replacement is important. Choose a deck mounted skylight system for steep-slope residential roofs where a low-profile appearance and integrated flashing are preferred.

The most important factors are roof compatibility, glazing performance, and professional installation quality. When these elements align, both skylight types can provide decades of reliable natural lighting and energy efficiency.
